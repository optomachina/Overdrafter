# Drawing Extraction

Last updated: October 20, 2026

## Purpose

This document defines the current drawing-metadata extraction rules for OverDrafter.

## Field priority

Extraction should prefer label-anchored title-block parsing over flat-text scanning.
The worker now uses a hybrid path:

1. deterministic title-block parsing first
2. bounded `gpt-5.4` fallback when critical fields are missing, low-confidence, or conflicting
3. validator and disagreement merge before persistence

Priority order per field:

1. explicit title-block labels such as `PART NUMBER`, `DWG. NO.`, `REV`, `DESCRIPTION`, `TITLE`, `MATERIAL`, `FINISH`, and `PROCESS`
2. values aligned to the same title-block row or the cell directly below the label
3. multiline continuation inside the same title-block region
4. field-specific fallback candidates inside the title block

## Field-specific guards

- Part number prefers company-style drawing numbers and rejects spec strings such as `MIL-A-8625F`, `ASTM`, `AMS`, `QQ-`, and approval or date metadata.
- Revision prefers values directly tied to `REV` or `REVISION` and rejects stray unlabeled letters from notes or revision-history blocks.
- Description prefers `TITLE` or `DESCRIPTION` regions, merges wrapped lines in reading order, and rejects numeric-only document identifiers when a labeled title exists.
- Finish prefers `FINISH` or `PROCESS` labels first, then finish-like note content only when it fits finish-specific keywords. Approval names, dates, and signature blocks must be rejected.
- A title-block label is never a value. `Q.A.`, `CHECKED`, `DRAWN`, `APPROVED`, `ENG`, `MFG` and the other labels in `titleBlockCells.ts` are rejected as a part number, revision, material or finish, whether they come from the title block, the filename stem, or a model.

## Title block cells (OVD-985, OVD-989)

The part number, revision, material and finish are the values of the cells their labels own (`worker/src/extraction/titleBlockCells.ts`). `pdftotext -layout` prints a title block row by row, so a cell's value is often several lines below its label with other cells' text in between; reading "the next line" lands on the approvals label `Q.A.`, the `TITLE` text, or the `SURFACE FINISH` note.

- Each line is split into labels from a fixed vocabulary and value runs. A label counts only where one can start (line start, after a wide gap, after another label, after a token with a digit on a line that already has a label), so `PLATING` inside "DIMENSIONS APPLY AFTER PLATING" and `REV` inside "PER ECO 1234 REV C" are not labels, and `ENG-1234` or `REV-B` are values. Labels that only fence cells off (`SHEET`, `SHT`, `PAGE`, `ECO`, the approvals labels, `SURFACE FINISH`, `UNLESS OTHERWISE SPECIFIED`, ...) are in the vocabulary so their text is never read as a value.
- A value belongs to a cell when it sits under its label: the value's centre is no more than a small slack left of the label's left edge, and the value starts no farther than a short reach right of the label's right edge (left aligned under the label, or centred in a cell that starts at it). The nearest such label on the left wins, and a label stops owning text below the first label under it in its own columns (`FINISH` under `MATERIAL`). The first value after a label on its own line (within a short gap), and the words that follow it, belong to that label. Text that sits in no label's cell is **never assigned**: a company name, a note or a zone letter leaves an empty cell empty. The sheet border's zone markers are dropped before the cells are read: a row of three or more single characters among the last lines of the block (`8 7 6 5 4 3 2`), and the one marker of that row that poppler prints on a line of its own just below it (`1`). A single character on the last line with no marker row above it is read like any other text, so a revision `A` under `REV` is the revision even when nothing follows it.
- A label the vocabulary does not know, set on the same row beside an empty cell (`HEAT TREAT` beside `MATERIAL`), is a fence and not that cell's value: the text after a label on its own line is another cell's label, and the field stays empty and flagged, when it holds a colon anywhere (`HEAT TREAT: RC 40-45`), when it starts with a word that heads another title block cell (`HEAT TREAT`, `HEAT TREATMENT`, `TREATMENT`, `HARDNESS`, `COLOR`, `PROCESS`, `SPEC`, `TEMPER`, `CONDITION`), or with one word of letters and then one of them other than `SPEC` (`SURFACE TREATMENT`, `PAINT COLOR`, `NO HEAT TREAT`; `PER SPEC` is a pointer) (a fence on that text only, not labels, so a value that runs under such a word keeps its cell), or when it has text of its own continuing underneath it, starting under it, that is taller (word boxes: a 6 pt label over a 9 pt value), that follows a blank line, or that sits under a short run of letters only (layout text, which has no heights). The value of another label on its own row is not text under this one, so a table of label and value rows (`MATERIAL STAINLESS STEEL` over `FINISH AS MACHINED`) is read. The inline window of a label with word boxes is also at most six heights of the label's own text, so a page set mostly in large dimensions does not widen it.
- A cell stacked under MATERIAL or FINISH in its columns (`HEAT TREAT` over `RC 40-45` between `MATERIAL` and `FINISH`, `SURFACE TREATMENT`, `PAINT` over `NONE`, `THICKNESS` over `.125`, `QTY 4`, `LOT CODE A123`) is owned by the cell above it, and its text is never read as a line of the value, whether or not the vocabulary knows its label and whatever size it is set in. What keeps it out is the shape of its line, not a list of labels: a label is words of letters, hyphenated or not (`HEAT-TREAT`, `X-RAY`), with a unit in brackets or a number sign among them (`THICKNESS (IN)`, `WT (LB)`, `LOT #`) but no digit or comma, alone on its line or followed by its own value. A first line of a material or finish gives no value (`unlisted_label`) when it starts with one of the words above (or `HEAT-TREAT`) or holds a colon; when a word after its first one is set apart from the words before it (two or more blanks, a cell break between word boxes) or, with word boxes, set taller than its first word, whatever the words are made of (`THICKNESS   .125`, a 6 pt `PAINT` by a 9 pt `RAL 9005`, a 6 pt `THICKNESS (IN)` or `HEAT-TREAT` by its 9 pt value, `# REQD   4`), since a value is printed in one run at one size; with word boxes, when text that no cell owns follows it on its line, right after it or before the next label or text a cell owns, set taller than its first word (a 6 pt `LOT #` by a 9 pt `A123` past the cell's reach); and when it is only label words with any other text in the cell, on its line or on a line under it, a single character included (`THICKNESS` or `THICKNESS (IN)` over `.125`, `QTY` over `4`, `SHEETS` over `1`, `THICKNESS` over `.5`, `ANODIZE` over `PAINT`), or, on layout text, with text that no cell owns after it on its line, at any size, since `ALUMINUM` over `6061-T6` is printed the same way and a single character never joins a value; and when it is only label words with text that no cell owns in the cell's rows, on a line between the label and it or within three lines under it, starting right of where it starts, within 40 columns of the label's end and before the next label or text another cell owns on that line, at any size, a single character included: a template that centres its values in wide cells prints the cell's own value past its label's reach, where no cell owns it, and the label of a cell stacked under it at the cell's left edge is then the only text the cell owns (`QTY` under a centred `AL 6061-T6`, or over a centred `4`). A later line that starts with words of letters is never joined as such: with word boxes, when those words are set smaller than the value (a 6 pt label under 9 pt text) and are shaped like a label (at most three words of letters, or one of the words above), the value ends above it, so `AL 6061-T6` over an empty `SURFACE TREATMENT` cell is the material alone and `ANODIZE` over `PAINT` / `BLACK` is the finish alone; otherwise the field is flagged (`AL 6061-T6` over a 9 pt `THICKNESS .125`, `ANODIZE, BLACK` over `QTY 4`, `THICKNESS (IN)`, `SURFACE ROUGHNESS (Ra)`, or an unknown `FROBNITZ`), on word boxes and on layout text alike. The one wrapped line that starts with words of letters and is still read is under a value printed beside its label on the label's own line: it starts where that value starts, a label's width from where the stacked cells' labels start, it goes on past its words of letters one word space later, and every word of it is set at the size of its first, which is the value's size (`CLASS 2` under `FINISH  ANODIZE, BLACK, MIL-A-8625F, TYPE II`, as the sample drawing prints it). Under a value that is under its label, `CLASS 2` cannot be told from `QTY 4`, so the field is flagged. A wrapped line that starts with anything else (`MIL-A-8625 TYPE II` under `ANODIZE, BLACK`) is read; a line set taller than the line above it makes that line a label over its own value, so the field is flagged; a line that starts with one of the words above or holds a colon is flagged, never cut short. A part number or revision is asked the same question in one form. Read from a line under its label (not beside it), an identifier that could be a label, only label words or, for a part number, no digit at all, gives no value (`unlisted_label`) when its cell holds other text: text the cell owns besides it, a single character included; on layout text, text that no cell owns after it on its line; with word boxes, taller text that no cell owns after it on its line; or text that no cell owns in the cell's rows, its own row and the label's included, starting right of where it starts, within 40 columns of the label's end and before the next label or text another cell owns on that line. A drawing number or revision centred past its label's reach, or printed along its label's row past the inline window (a row-form table), belongs to no cell, and the label of an unlisted cell stacked under it at the cell's left edge (`QTY`, `ITEM`, `STOCK`, `CUSTOMER`) is then the only text the cell owns; it is flagged, not read as the identifier. A lone revision letter is not label words, and `AB`, `NC` or `ABC-DEF` with nothing else in its cell is read; an identifier with a digit is never asked.
- The OCR rescue (`rescueOcrTitleBlockFields`) never approves a material or finish. Its text for the sample drawing (`6061 Alloy`, `ANODIZE, BLACK, MIL-A-8625F, TYPE II CLASS 2`) can sit anywhere on a sheet, in a note as well as in the cell, so approving it would bypass the cell the label owns. In a field the cell reader rejected or flagged, or one with no label, the text is only a suggestion at confidence 0.2, flagged for review; a value the cell reader approved is kept and flagged when the rescue's text disagrees with it.
- A colon ends a label whatever follows it, so a label and its value printed as one word (`MATERIAL:ALUMINUM`, `DWG NO:ABC-001`, `REV:A`) are the label and its inline value. With word boxes poppler prints such a word as one box; the part of the word after the label is a run of its own, placed in the box by its share of the word's characters.
- A wrapped value continues on the line under it only when that line overlaps its columns and holds more than one character; under a material or finish that is only label words, a single character in the cell flags the field instead (see above). With word boxes the words after the first one that overlaps continue along their own line even where they run past the end of the line above (`ANODIZE, BLACK` over `MIL-A-8625 TYPE III` is read whole), and a word the cell owns that the value did not take, on a line the value was read from (far along the line, in a wide cell), leaves the field empty and flagged, so a value is not cut short. Two values for a single-valued field, text on the label's own line together with text left aligned under the label (it may be another cell's label and value), or the label of another field inside the value (two cells run together on one line) give no value.
- Fail closed: a label with no value, with a value that is itself a label (also when spelled with look-alike or invisible characters), with a single character (a sheet-border zone marker), with a material or finish that points elsewhere (`SEE NOTE 1`, `SEE BOM`, `PER SPEC`, `AS NOTED`; a specification followed by its designator, `PER SPECIFICATION MIL-DTL-5541`, names the finish and is read) or stops in the middle (`ANODIZE, CLASS`, a trailing comma, hyphen or bracket), or with a part number containing a space (two cells squeezed together) yields no value. The field is flagged for review and left empty; no fallback reads neighbouring text. A part number shaped like `1093-05589` found elsewhere in the title block is still offered as a flagged suggestion, and a company-style number is recognised only as the whole value (a match inside `XYZ-1093-05589` does not replace it). Identical candidates from different sheets agree; different ones are flagged.
- Geometry: when `pdftotext -bbox` word boxes are available (`words` on each page), the reader uses the words' true positions. `-layout` columns are squeezed where a word is set larger than its neighbours (a 24 pt drawing number beside 9 pt labels prints as `B  ABC-001 A`, with the revision no longer under `REV`). Word positions are measured in columns of 0.4 text heights (the median word height of the page), so a sheet drawn at another size reads the same, and the size poppler gives a rotated page is never used. Lines of word boxes are numbered in order, and each line's distance down the page is measured in text heights too, the empty stretch of the sheet included: a value more than 20 text heights under its label is not its value, and a line that would continue a value but is more than 3 text heights under it flags the field (`far_value`), however few lines of text lie between (text 400 points under `MATERIAL` in its columns is not the material). The checks that flag a cell for text near its value count the lines in order, so a sparse sheet does not put that text out of their reach. Layout text has no such measure: poppler prints at most four blank lines between two lines of text however far apart they are, so a value under four blank lines is at a distance the text does not give and is not read (`far_value`). Without boxes the reader uses layout columns (a character each, with a wider reach, since another poppler version places the same text at other columns), and the squeezed value is flagged rather than read. Layout text cannot tell the rest of a value from a neighbouring cell when the pitch is fine (`pdftotext -fixed 1`: the words of one value are tens of columns apart), so on layout text a material or finish is flagged when text that no cell owns follows it on its line within 80 columns or when a run that belongs to another label's band follows it directly, and a cell of any of the four fields is flagged when it owns a run its value did not take, when a run starts under one label and has its centre under another or runs on into the columns of the next label (two cells printed on one line at a pitch too coarse to keep them apart: a drawing number and its revision, a finish and the material beside it), or when text that no label claims stands just above its label (a coarse pitch prints the lines of a stack out of order); and every cell of a page whose own labels are spread apart (`DWG.   NO.`) is flagged. Poppler 24.02 (CI) and 26.10 lay the same page out into different cells at the same `-fixed` pitch, so the tests read it at every half unit from 0.5 to 24.
- Bounds: a line over 2,000 characters, a page of over 400 lines, 400 labels or 2,000 runs is not read; a line over 2,000 characters is still a line of the page, so a cell whose text could be on it (under its label, before the next label in its columns and within 20 lines, or on layout text up to 3 lines above the label) is not read across it and the field is flagged (`unread_line`): the line may hold the label that ends the cell (`FINISH` under `MATERIAL`) or the rest of the value; each loop stops at its own bound, and the cost of a line is linear in its length (no end-anchored pattern on an unbounded string). Word boxes are kept for at most 20,000 words per page and 60,000 words per document, and only from complete `-bbox` markup.

Known limits (each one leaves the field empty and flagged, or reads one line of text that nothing in the page tells from the cell's own value): a lone letter or number inside the narrow reach of the `REV` label cannot be told from a revision; unlabelled text directly under an empty label, inside its reach, is read as that cell's value; labels centred over left-aligned values are not paired; a label split over two lines (`DWG.` over `NO.`) is not a label; a 24 pt drawing number whose box runs into the `REV` cell leaves the revision flagged; an unlisted label whose value under it is as tall as the label (word boxes) is not told from a wrapped value; an unlisted label that is not one of the neighbour cell words above and has no colon in its text, alone in an empty MATERIAL or FINISH with nothing under it (beside the label, or under it with the next label of the stack directly below: `LOT CODE` alone, `PAINT` alone), or printed with its own value one word space after it at the same size (`LOT CODE A123`, or `THICKNESS .125` on layout text), is read as that cell's value, because one line of that shape is also how `AS MACHINED`, `STAINLESS STEEL` or `AL 6061-T6` is printed (it is never joined to a value); a stacked cell set at the value's size whose label starts exactly where a value printed beside its own label starts, and is followed one word space later by its value (`QTY 4` under `FINISH  ANODIZE, BLACK`), is read as the value's wrapped line, the shape of `CLASS 2` on the sample drawing; a material or finish whose first line layout text prints in two runs (two or more blanks between its words) is flagged, as the price of telling `PAINT   BLACK` from a value; a material or finish of label words only with a stray single character in its cell (a zone marker under `AS MACHINED`), or on layout text after it on its line, is flagged, as the price of telling `QTY` over `4` from a value, and so is one with text that no cell owns within 40 columns right of it on a line under it or between it and its label (a note or a company name set close beside the cell), as the price of seeing a value centred past its label's reach; a value centred farther than that, more than 40 columns past its label's end (about 130 points on a sheet of 9 pt text), is not seen, so the label of a cell stacked under it at the cell's left edge is read as the value (`QTY`), as with an empty cell, and the same holds for a drawing number or revision; a part number or revision of letters only (`ABC-DEF`, `AB`) with other text in its cell, or text that no cell owns within 40 columns right of it on its own row, its label's row or a row between or just under them, is flagged, as the price of telling a stacked `QTY` from the identifier; on layout text alone, a value under four blank lines is flagged even when it is near, which is how poppler prints a 24 pt drawing number under its 6 pt label when the cells of the rows between are empty; a revision that is the last line of a block is read even when it is a stray border marker, unless the marker row is the line above it.

## General tolerance table rows (OVD-989)

A block such as `TWO PLACE DECIMAL .01 / THREE PLACE DECIMAL .005 / FOUR PLACE DECIMAL .0010` (also `.XX ±.01`, and metric `X.XX 0.10` where the plus-minus glyph is not in the text layer) is a default for dimensions printed with that many decimal places. Reading its tightest row as the drawing's tightest tolerance denied every drawing from a template that prints a four-place row although no dimension uses four places.

A row is dropped on positive evidence alone, and a number that was not seen is never evidence, so the rule reads a value tighter than the part's, or the part's own, wherever it sees the dimensions. What it does not see is read as main reads it (the limits below).

- **Readable (T1).** The whole drawing was read (`drawingFullyRead`: the page count is known, and every page's text layer and OCR were read; a page that was not read may print the dimension that keeps a row), the text layer is not OCR, no line was too long to scan, and at least three decimal numbers sit on lines that hold only dimension callouts (numbers, `2X`, `R`, `Ø`, `THRU`, hole tags `A` to `H` or a letter with digits). The gate is strict on purpose; a miss only makes the sheet unreadable.
- **Evidence (T2).** On a readable sheet the evidence is the most decimal places printed anywhere in the text, counting every decimal number except the values of the table's own rows. No line is skipped and no number is dropped for what is glued to it (`Ø.1875`, `n.1875`, `SR.0625`, `.1875A`, `.1875THRU`, `1.2500`), so a dimension that shares a layout line with a note, a view label, a weight, the scale cell or the table itself still counts. A row applies unless every decimal number printed has fewer places than the row. An explicit tolerance on a feature is always kept. Metric rows follow the same rule. A decimal the text layer garbled counts as the places it may carry, so it cannot be what drops its own row: a separator, one blank, then at least three digits (`Ø. 1875`, `HOLE 3. 1750`), digits split by one blank (`.18 75`), a mantissa and an exponent (`1.875E-1`), and a separator spelled with another character (a middle dot, a bullet, the Arabic decimal separator, an ideographic full stop) is a separator. A text layer garbles a decimal by one blank, not by a layout column, and ordinary lines of a drawing must not look like a garbled decimal (each once kept the four-place row, `0.001`, on drawings from the template, and the OVD-683 beta gate denied them), so these count only with a single blank, with nothing continuing the digits (a letter, a degree sign, a digit or a point: `.500   2X`, `.50 45°`, `Ø. 1875A`), and not when the separator is the point after a note number at the start of a line (`2. 1018 STEEL`), a written date (`10. 29. 2013`, `OCT 19, 2026`) or a specification and its year (`ASTM B633, 2019`); two letters before a look-alike separator make a name (`ACME·1234`). The price is the safe direction: a dimension and an integer one blank apart on a layout line (`.75 12`) still reads as a split decimal and keeps its row.
- **Unreadable (T3).** On a sheet that is not readable, a row of four or more places is not assumed in force: it becomes an unresolved value (the OVD-976 policy), so the stored `valueInch` is null unless a tighter explicit tolerance exists and the OVD-683 beta gate asks the user. Such a row keeps every safeguard any other value has: one in scientific notation (`FOUR PLACE DECIMAL 1e-3`) is not read by its mantissa and blocks, and a per-length ratio or a decimal comma is read at its smallest reading, in the unit written after it, as any other value is. Rows of one to three places are read as before.
- **Raster (T4).** Every page with a text layer is also OCR'd, because a raster view can sit beside vector text. OCR digits are trusted neither to drop a row nor to set one in force. When the OCR text prints, as a dimension callout (`2X Ø.1875 THRU`, or `Ø.1875` on a line with a view label, `Ø.1875 THRU SECTION A-A`), a decimal the text layer does not print anywhere as the same whole number, with more places than T2 found, the text layer did not show every dimension: the sheet is then read as T3 reads one, so a row of more places than both T2's evidence and three is an unresolved value (`valueInch` null unless a tighter explicit tolerance exists), never dropped. A decimal the text layer also prints (the table's own `.0010`, every vector dimension OCR reads again) is no evidence; the whole number decides, so `Ø1.0010` is not the table's `.0010`. Neither is a line of the table or the notes, nor a number glued to other text, where OCR garbles them (`.005` read as `, 5005`).
- A number that is not a dimension (a weight, an edge-break note, a surface finish) with as many places as a row therefore keeps that row. That is the safe error: the tolerance read is tighter than the part's, never looser.
- Units are untouched: bare row values follow the drawing's declared units (OVD-976).

Known limits: a dimension that is in neither the text layer nor the OCR text (outlined text OCR misses), or that OCR reads with no more places than the text layer prints, glued to other text, or as exactly a number the text layer prints, is not seen; a limit dimension (`.5005/.5000`) and an unequal bilateral tolerance (`+.0005/-.0000`) are not read as an explicit tolerance of their own, and a table row whose value is missing takes the next dimension for its value (`FOUR PLACE DECIMAL` followed by `.1875 THRU`), on this branch as on main.

## Page text and OCR

`extractPdfText` reads the text layer of every page with `pdftotext` and also renders and OCRs every page with `tesseract`, even when a text layer exists. A drawing can have a selectable title block next to an embedded raster image, such as a marking stamp, that has no text layer. Reading only the text layer, or only page 1, would treat that drawing as fully read.

- Each page carries `text` (text layer, or the OCR text when the whole document has no text layer, as before), `ocrText` (OCR output only, never mixed with the text layer), and `ocrStatus`.
- `ocrStatus` is one of:
  - `ok`: the page was rendered and tesseract ran on it. The text may be empty.
  - `failed`: the page size could not be read, the page is over the pixel cap, or the render or tesseract errored or timed out.
  - `unavailable`: the tesseract binary is missing, or its `--version` probe failed or timed out.
  - `not_read`: the page is past `PDF_OCR_MAX_PAGES`, or the total OCR budget ran out before the next call for that page could start. No tool ran to completion on that page, so nothing about its content is known.
- Only `ok` means the raster content of the page was read.
- `words` (OVD-989) holds the text layer's word boxes in points, from `pdftotext -bbox`, for each page that has a text layer. It is geometry for the title block reader and nothing else: it is absent when poppler cannot print the boxes (or prints markup that stops before the end of the document), when the page has more than `MAX_WORDS_PER_PAGE` words, or when the document's pages together have more than `MAX_WORDS_TOTAL`, and its absence never changes `text`, `drawingFullyRead` or the export scan.
- `ocrLimit` is set only when a fixed limit kept the page from being read, so no rerun of the same bytes can read it: `page_cap` (`not_read`, past `PDF_OCR_MAX_PAGES`) or `pixel_cap` (`failed`, over `PDF_OCR_MAX_PIXELS`). A timeout, a spent budget, or a tool error sets no limit. The export gate records such a drawing as final (`drawing_too_many_pages`, `drawing_sheet_too_large`).
- `pageCountKnown` is false when `pdfinfo` failed or printed no page count. `pageCount` is then a floor of 1, and later pages may exist unread.
- `drawingFullyRead` is the single fail closed answer for part B and any export gate. It is true only when `pageCountKnown` is true, the text layer of every page was read without error, and every page has `ocrStatus` `ok`. Anything else must hold the drawing. `ocr` keeps its older meaning: no text layer anywhere and OCR produced text.
- Each page is rendered at no less than `PDF_OCR_MIN_DPI` (200) so small print stays legible. The render covers the page CropBox (`pdftoppm -cropbox`), which is the size `pdfinfo` reports, and a page whose CropBox would exceed `PDF_OCR_MAX_PIXELS` (70 MP) at that resolution is `failed` instead of rendered. An E size sheet at 200 DPI is about 60 MP. Rendering the CropBox keeps the checked size and the rendered size the same, so a small CropBox on a huge MediaBox cannot slip past the cap.
- tesseract runs with `OMP_THREAD_LIMIT=1`. On a loaded host more threads were many times slower and could hit the page timeout.
- Time bounds:
  - each `pdfinfo` and `pdftotext` call, and the tesseract version probe: `PDF_TOOL_TIMEOUT_MS` (30 seconds);
  - each OCR render and each tesseract run: `PDF_OCR_PAGE_TIMEOUT_MS` (60 seconds);
  - the whole OCR pass: `PDF_OCR_TOTAL_TIMEOUT_MS` (120 seconds). The clock starts before the version probe. Immediately before each `pdfinfo`, `pdftoppm`, and tesseract call the time left is computed, the call gets at most that, and when none is left the call is skipped and the page reports `not_read`;
  - the text layer loop: `PDF_TEXT_LAYER_MAX_PAGES` (50 pages) and `PDF_TEXT_LAYER_TOTAL_TIMEOUT_MS` (60 seconds). Pages it does not reach have an empty `text`, and `drawingFullyRead` is false;
  - the word box pass: `PDF_WORD_BOXES_TOTAL_TIMEOUT_MS` (30 seconds), each call also bounded by `PDF_TOOL_TIMEOUT_MS`. Pages it does not reach keep their layout text and have no `words`;
  - the preview and title block crop renders: `PDF_OCR_PAGE_TIMEOUT_MS`.
- The title block crop also renders the CropBox, since its crop rectangle is computed from the size `pdfinfo` reports. The page size parser accepts size lines that end with a paper name, such as `612 x 792 pts (letter)`, so the crop now works for letter, legal, and A series pages.
- If the tesseract binary is missing, every page reports `unavailable` and the function does not throw. It logs the fixed code `pdf_ocr_tesseract_unavailable` once and never logs file names or contents.
- The worker image installs `tesseract-ocr` and the English data at pinned distro versions. Nothing is downloaded at runtime.

## Raw vs normalized data

- `drawing_extractions.extraction` is the source-truth record.
- Raw extracted fields include `extractedPartNumberRaw`, `extractedRevisionRaw`, `extractedDescriptionRaw`, and `extractedFinishRaw`.
- Quote-facing normalized fields live in `approved_part_requirements` plus `spec_snapshot`.
- `spec_snapshot.quoteDescription` and `spec_snapshot.quoteFinish` can compress or rephrase drawing text for quote workflows, but they must remain traceable to the raw extraction.
- Reparse must not overwrite normalized fields when provenance marks them as user-managed.
- Legacy `approved_part_requirements` rows with missing `spec_snapshot.fieldSources.*` must be treated as auto-managed, not user-managed.
- Auto-managed approved values should only be refreshed from extraction when the extraction is newer than the approved row and the extracted raw field is not review-blocked.

## Confidence and review

- Every extracted field carries confidence and `reviewNeeded`.
- Low-confidence or conflicting candidates must fail closed into review instead of silently becoming approved requirement data.
- Debug mode should log top candidates, rejection reasons, selected value, and source region.
- Model fallback must persist provenance such as `modelFallbackUsed`, `modelName`, `modelPromptVersion`, `fieldSelections`, and model candidate metadata without replacing parser `debugCandidates`.

## Model fallback rules

- The model receives the title-block crop first and the full first-page render only when the crop result is insufficient.
- The model returns raw drawing truth only, never quote-normalized strings.
- Parser-selected fields remain authoritative when they are strong, label-backed, and conflict-free.
- Model-selected fields may rescue missing or weak parser values, but they still pass field-specific validation.
- If parser and model disagree on a critical field and neither clearly wins, the field stays review-needed and the extraction lifecycle remains partial.
- The worker should still try to produce a first-page preview for model fallback when Poppler text extraction is unavailable. On macOS debug hosts, Quick Look rendering is an acceptable fallback for this preview image.

## Extraction Lab

- Internal debugging now supports preview-only reruns through the `Extraction Lab` on the internal job detail page.
- Debug reruns enqueue `debug_extract_part` tasks and persist their result in `debug_extraction_runs`.
- Preview-only runs must not overwrite canonical `drawing_extractions` rows or `approved_part_requirements`.
- The lab should show canonical extraction and the latest preview-only debug run side by side, including:
  - worker build version
  - extractor version
  - requested model and effective model
  - raw extracted fields
  - normalized quote-facing fields
  - review fields, warnings, and candidate/debug metadata
- The browser must request preview runs through Supabase RPC rather than calling the worker directly.
- `OPENAI_API_KEY` or `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` belongs only in the worker environment or secret manager.
- When OpenRouter is the active provider, an unqualified model such as `gpt-5.4` is sent as the provider-qualified `openai/gpt-5.4`; already-qualified model identifiers are preserved.
- `DRAWING_EXTRACTION_DEBUG_ALLOWED_MODELS` defines the allowlisted per-run model selector shown in the lab.
- `WORKER_BUILD_VERSION` should be injected from deploy metadata or git SHA so preview runs and canonical rows reveal the exact worker build that produced them.

## Internal review precedence

- Internal quote review should not blindly display `approved_part_requirements` when those values were auto-derived.
- If an approved field is auto-managed, the extraction is newer, and the extracted field is not review-blocked, the review UI should display the fresher extraction-backed value and surface the stale approved value as provenance.
- Explicit user-owned approved values remain authoritative until a user chooses to rebuild them from extraction.
- If there is no approved value yet and the extraction is review-blocked, the editable field should stay blank while the raw extracted candidate remains visible as review evidence.

## Smoke verification

- For real-file diagnostics outside fixture coverage, use `npm --prefix worker run extract:smoke -- /absolute/path/to/drawing.pdf`.
- The smoke command should print the raw extraction payload, preview path, and run directory so parser-vs-model behavior can be inspected against a production PDF without mutating application data.
- For app-level debugging of a stored part, prefer the internal `Extraction Lab` first because it keeps the run attached to the part/job record, records build/model metadata, and avoids reuploading or archiving files.

## Regression coverage

Regression coverage must include the `1093-05589` title-block layout:

- raw part number: `1093-05589`
- raw revision: `02`
- raw description: `ROUND, CARBON FIBER END ATTACHMENTS BONDED`
- raw finish: `ANODIZE, BLACK, MIL-A-8625F, TYPE II CLASS 2`

The same coverage should assert that:

- `MIL-A-8625F` does not win as part number
- approval metadata does not win as finish
- numeric-only stray text does not beat a labeled description
- quote normalization remains separate from raw extraction

The deterministic path is also run, under a neutral file name, on the committed sample drawing through poppler (`sampleDrawing.test.ts`) and on synthetic B-size title blocks (`titleBlockLayout.test.ts`, `titleBlockCells.test.ts`, `titleBlockWords.test.ts`, `generalToleranceRows.test.ts`). They assert that the part number and revision come from the `DWG. NO.` and `REV` cells, that `Q.A.` and the other approvals labels are never values, that the material and finish never come from the `TITLE` cell or the `SURFACE FINISH` note, that text belonging to no label is never assigned, and that a four-place tolerance row no dimension uses does not set the tightest tolerance while a four-place dimension anywhere keeps it. `syntheticPdf.test.ts` writes small PDFs (`worker/test-support/minimalPdf.ts`) and reads them through poppler, including a sheet drawn at other sizes and a page stored with `/Rotate 90`; `titleBlockCells.hostile.test.ts` and the hostile blocks of `generalToleranceRows.test.ts` and `pdfDrawing.test.ts` pin each cap and the linear cost. Real SolidWorks drawings are not committed; the repository is public.

## extract:eval CLI tool

`extract:eval` is a command-line evaluation harness for benchmarking and comparing drawing-extraction accuracy across multiple AI models and providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, and OpenRouter).

Basic usage:

```bash
npm --prefix worker run extract:eval -- /path/to/drawing.pdf
```

The tool runs the extraction prompt against the configured model(s), prints a structured comparison of field values, confidence scores, token usage, and estimated cost per model, and exits without writing to the database. It is intended for offline accuracy measurement against known-good drawings rather than live job processing.
